## What four green booleans actually mean

> Of the 4 governed registration conditions, NH can verify 1 mechanically (challenge-response over a nonce NH chose). The other 3 are recorded claims by the NH operator: NH refuses to activate until they are claimed and makes the claim auditable, and it establishes none of them itself. A registered key is therefore NOT a verified institution, and no readiness level above SOURCE_ATTESTED is reachable from this ceremony.

`keyMayBeActivated` refuses to activate a key until all four conditions are claimed, and `keyRegistrationRefusals` returns **every** unmet one rather than the first — so a registration blocked on two grounds says so. But the booleans arrive from the NH operator and the server validates only that they are `true`. The register's integrity is strong; **its input is a human claim**, and a pilot brief presenting four ticks as four verified facts would overstate exactly what this checklist exists to prevent.

Mechanically verifiable conditions: **keyControlDemonstrated**.

## C1 · the channel is the institution's own, found independently

Governed field: `channelIndependentlyVerified`

### K5-E1

- **Collect:** The institution's own published key-distribution channel, located WITHOUT using any address, link or contact supplied in the message that carried the key — e.g. a URL reached from the institution's primary domain, or a channel named in a signed contract already on file.
- **Issued by:** the institution, published independently of this exchange
- **NH's position:** NH RECORDS IT AND ESTABLISHES NOTHING — a human verification
- **Why this and not something cheaper:** The attack this defeats is the whole reason the condition exists: an attacker who controls the message that delivers the key also controls every address inside it, so verifying the channel against the message verifies the attacker against himself.
- **Does NOT establish:** that the key published there is the key the institution intended — only that the channel is the institution's own.

### K5-E2

- **Collect:** A record of HOW the channel was located, naming the independent source used (domain, contract clause, regulator register entry) and the date.
- **Issued by:** the NH operator performing the ceremony
- **NH's position:** NH checks it is PRESENT AND COMPLETE — not that it is true
- **Why this and not something cheaper:** Without it the channel check is unrepeatable, and an unrepeatable check cannot be audited later by anyone who was not in the room.
- **Does NOT establish:** that the independent source was itself genuine.

## C2 · the institution is who it says it is

Governed field: `institutionIdentityVerified`

### K5-E3

- **Collect:** The legal entity's identity from an authority that is NOT a party to this exchange: company registration number and legal name from the companies registry, or the licence entry from the financial regulator that supervises it.
- **Issued by:** a companies registry or financial regulator
- **NH's position:** NH RECORDS IT AND ESTABLISHES NOTHING — a human verification
- **Why this and not something cheaper:** This is the one condition a challenge-response can never supply. Anyone can generate an Ed25519 pair and answer a nonce perfectly; what that proves is CONTROL OF A KEY and nothing about WHO HOLDS IT. The identity has to come from somewhere neither side of the exchange controls.
- **Does NOT establish:** that the person who sent the key is authorised by that entity — see K5-E4.

### K5-E4

- **Collect:** Confirmation that the individual or system supplying the key is authorised by the institution to do so, obtained through the channel from K5-E1 and NOT through the sender's own reply path.
- **Issued by:** the institution, through its own published channel
- **NH's position:** NH RECORDS IT AND ESTABLISHES NOTHING — a human verification
- **Why this and not something cheaper:** An entity being real does not make a particular sender its agent, and a compromised mailbox inside a genuine institution is the most ordinary form of this attack.
- **Does NOT establish:** that the key itself is uncompromised at the institution's end.

## C3 · the responder controls the key being registered

Governed field: `keyControlDemonstrated`

### K5-E5

- **Collect:** A challenge-response: NH chooses a fresh random nonce, the institution returns a signature over it under the key being registered, and NH verifies that signature against the public key it is about to register. The nonce, the response and the verification result are all recorded.
- **Issued by:** the institution, under the key being registered
- **NH's position:** **NH VERIFIES THIS** — mechanically, from the artefact
- **Why this and not something cheaper:** The ONLY cryptographic step in the ceremony and the only item NH can verify mechanically. It proves the responder holds the private half of the key about to be registered.
- **Does NOT establish:** WHO holds it. This item may never substitute for K5-E3 or K5-E4, which is why the lifecycle declares `keyControlDemonstrated` as a field separate from `institutionIdentityVerified`.

### K5-E6

- **Collect:** The public key's fingerprint, read back to the institution through the K5-E1 channel and confirmed there, so the value NH registers is the value the institution meant to publish.
- **Issued by:** the institution, through its own published channel
- **NH's position:** NH RECORDS IT AND ESTABLISHES NOTHING — a human verification
- **Why this and not something cheaper:** A challenge-response succeeds against whatever key was substituted along with the challenge. Reading the fingerprint back through an independent channel is what closes the substitution.
- **Does NOT establish:** anything about the key's future handling at the institution.

## C4 · the ceremony is recorded, by two identities

Governed field: `ceremonyRecorded`

### K5-E7

- **Collect:** The ceremony record: the channel used, who confirmed at the institution, the key fingerprint, the declared validity window, the nonce and response from K5-E5, the date, and the two NH identities — the one who proposed and the one who activated.
- **Issued by:** the NH operator performing the ceremony
- **NH's position:** NH checks it is PRESENT AND COMPLETE — not that it is true
- **Why this and not something cheaper:** The register enforces that the proposer is not the activator (`KEY_PROPOSER_MAY_NOT_ACTIVATE`), and this is the human half of that control. Without the record the separation is a database fact with no account of what the two people actually did.
- **Does NOT establish:** that the steps it describes were performed correctly — it is a record, and NH checks that it is complete, not that it is true.

### K5-E8

- **Collect:** A documented approval by a second authorised NH identity, distinct from whoever performed K5-E1 to K5-E6, recorded before activation.
- **Issued by:** the second NH identity (the activator)
- **NH's position:** NH checks it is PRESENT AND COMPLETE — not that it is true
- **Why this and not something cheaper:** No beneficiary may be the sole author, approver or verifier of their own claim. The register refuses a proposer who tries to activate, so this item is what that refusal is protecting.
- **Does NOT establish:** independence of judgement — only that two identities were involved.

## What is still blocked after every item above is collected

Nothing here reaches `AUTHORITY_VERIFIED`. That rung means *evidenced by a channel the beneficiary cannot unilaterally alter*, and this ceremony is performed and recorded by the submitting side. The four channels that would reach it — a signed export, a fetch NH performs itself, a machine-issued system-of-record attestation, and third-party reconciliation — are unbuilt, and only the last has a corroborating party that is not party to the claim.

**And the item this checklist cannot supply:** if an institution will not sign an attestation at all, there is no externally attested settlement and the correct output is **no PROVEN claim** — not a lower confidence, not a provisional proof. Claimed revenue is not proven revenue.
